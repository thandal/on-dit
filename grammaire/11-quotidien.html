Chapter 11Everyday frames

Numbers, prices, times, dates and quantities follow fixed patterns, and so do greetings and polite phrases. Several numbers change their last sound depending on what comes next, so this chapter has more stars than most.

Before a noun

Counted on their own, six, huit and dix say their last consonant. Before a noun, they follow the normal rules: silent before a consonant (1.5), linked before a vowel (1.6). So they need no star there.

cinq and sept keep their last consonant everywhere: {sink*|cinq} livres, {sète*|sept} euros. neuf says its f, except before ans (years) and heures (hours), where it becomes v: {neuv‿ans*|neuf ans}, {neuv‿heures*|neuf heures}.

11.3 Prices

  • ça coûte combien ? (how much does it cost?) · ça fait combien ? (how much is that altogether?)
  • c'est dix‿euros · ça fait {sète*|sept} euros cinquante (€7.50). The cents follow the euros, with no word for "and".
  • je peux payer par carte ? (can I pay by card?) · c'est gratuit (it's free)

For schedules, opening hours and appointments, people use the 24-hour clock with plain minutes: le train part à {dissète*|dix-sept} heures trente (the train leaves at 17:30). In conversation, the 12-hour clock is usual, with du matin (in the morning), de l'après-midi or du soir (in the evening) if needed.

11.7 Greetings and everyday courtesy

Each table gives the everyday form first. The polite form is for strangers, shops and anyone you say vous to (3.2).

Saying hello

In France, say bonjour when you walk into a shop, a café or a waiting room, and to anyone before asking them something. Starting with a question and no bonjour comes across as rude.

More: la bise

Friends, family and often colleagues greet each other with la bise: a light kiss on each cheek, or just cheek to cheek. Men who are close friends or family do it too; otherwise men usually shake hands. The number of kisses varies by region, two being the most common. If you're unsure, let the other person lead.

What you'll hear:

  • et avec ceci ? (anything else?) · ce sera tout ? (will that be all?)
  • sur place ou à emporter ? (eat in or take away?)
  • vous payez comment ? par carte ? (how are you paying? by card?) · {sans contacte*|sans contact} ? (contactless?)
  • vous voulez le ticket ? (do you want the receipt?)

Shops in France don't print a receipt unless you want one, so you may need to ask: je peux avoir le ticket, s'il vous plaît ? To turn one down: non merci, c'est bon (no thanks, I'm fine).
