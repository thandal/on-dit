Chapter 6Verbs: other common forms

Seven more ways to talk about time and possibility, from the second half of the map (5.1). They come up less often than the everyday four, but you'll hear them all: je voudrais (I'd like), il faut qu'on parte (we have to go), on verra (we'll see). Most combine pieces from chapters 4 and 5. The irregular parts to learn are a dozen stems (6.3) and six subjunctives (6.5).

6.1 venir de, être en train de: have just, be in the middle of

Two more combinations work like the futur proche (5.6): a verb in the present, plus an infinitive.

  • venir de + infinitive for what has just happened: il vient de partir (he's just left), je viens de manger (I've just eaten).
  • être en train de + infinitive to stress that something is in progress: {chui*|je suis} en train de manger (I'm eating right now). Without that stress, the present does the job: je mange (I'm eating).

In a story about the past, both go into the imparfait (5.5): il venait de partir (he had just left), j'étais en train de manger (I was in the middle of eating).

désolé, je viens de voir ton message

Sorry, I've just seen your message. · venir de + infinitive

Practice: venir de, être en train de
  1. I've just arrived. je viens d'arriver
  2. She's just left. elle vient de partir
  3. We're in the middle of eating. on‿est en train de manger
  4. He had just left. il venait de partir

6.2 Plus-que-parfait: what had already happened

The passé composé (5.4) with its helper moved into the imparfait (5.5). You already have both pieces:

  • j'ai oublié (I forgot) → j'avais oublié (I had forgotten)
  • il est parti (he left) → il était déjà parti (he had already left)
  • on s'est pas compris → on s'était pas compris (we hadn't understood each other)

The choice of avoir or être is the same as in the passé composé.

6.3 Conditionnel: what would happen

Take the infinitive and end it in "rè", or "rié" with vous: parler → "parl'rè", finir → "finirè", prendre → "prandrè". The written forms:

Infinitiveje, tu, il, on, ilsvous
parlerje parlerais ("parl'rè") · ils {parlerè*|parleraient}vous parleriez
finirje finiraisvous finiriez
prendreje prendraisvous prendriez

So the conditionnel always has an r before the ending. That r is what tells je parlerais (I would speak) from the imparfait je parlais (I was speaking; 5.5).

Conditionnel passé: what would have happened

One more combination from pieces you have: avoir or être in the conditionnel, plus the participle.

  • j'aurais dû (I should have), j'aurais pu (I could have), j'aurais aimé (I would have liked)
  • si j'avais su, je serais venu (if I'd known, I would have come)
Practice: conditionnel
  1. parler → je ___ je parlerais
  2. pouvoir → tu ___ m'aider ? tu pourrais m'aider ?
  3. aller → si j'avais le temps, j'___ j'irais
  4. être → ce ___ bien ce serait bien
  5. devoir → on ___ partir on devrait partir
  6. I should have called. (devoir, appeler) j'aurais dû appeler

6.4 Futur simple: what will happen

French also has a one-word future. The futur proche (5.6) is the safe default, but you'll hear the futur simple all the time, especially:

  • in promises and predictions: je t'appellerai (I'll call you), tu verras (you'll see), ça ira (it'll be OK)
  • in negatives: je le ferai pas (I won't do it), ça changera rien (it won't change anything)
  • after quand, about the future, where English uses the present: quand tu seras grand (when you grow up). The present is heard here too: appelle-moi quand tu arrives (10.3).

It uses the same stem as the conditionnel (6.3): the infinitive, or one of the irregular stems (je serai, j'irai, on verra, il faudra). Only the endings differ:

jetu, il, onvousils
je parleraitu parleras · on parleravous parlerezils parleront

For many speakers, je parlerai sounds like the conditionnel je parlerais. The other forms are clearly different: on parlera (we'll speak) / on parlerait (we'd speak).

Some fixed phrases to learn as they are: on verra (we'll see), ce sera (it will be), on‿en reparlera (we'll talk about it later).

t'inquiète, je t'appellerai demain

Don't worry, I'll call you tomorrow. · appeler, futur simple, je: a promise

Practice: futur simple
  1. (voir) Tu ___ ! (you'll see) tu verras
  2. (aller) Ça ___. (it'll be OK) ça ira
  3. (faire) Je le ___ pas. (I won't do it) je le ferai pas
  4. (être) Quand tu ___ grand… quand tu seras grand
  5. (appeler) Je t'___ demain. je t'appellerai demain

6.5 Subjunctive

When: after a short list of phrases. The main ones are il faut que (it's necessary that), je veux que (I want…to), j'aimerais que (I'd like…to), pour que (so that), avant que (before), bien que (although) and ça m'étonnerait que (I'd be surprised if). The subjunctive rarely adds meaning of its own. It's required by the phrase, so learn the phrases (full list).

How it sounds: for almost every verb, the subjunctive with je, tu, il, on or ils sounds like the present-tense ils form (4.2):

ils (present)Subjunctive
ils {parte*|partent}il faut qu'on parte
ils {finisse*|finissent}il faut que je finisse
ils {prenne*|prennent}il faut que tu prennes
ils {vienne*|viennent}j'aimerais qu'il vienne
ils {parle*|parlent}il faut qu'on parle

For one-form verbs (4.1), which includes every -er verb, the ils form sounds like the je form. So il faut que je parle sounds just like the present je parle, and you'll often use the subjunctive without noticing.

With vous, use the imparfait vous form (5.5): il faut que vous partiez.

Six common verbs have their own subjunctive, and these you have to learn. Learn them with on:

il faut qu'on fasse les courses

We need to do the shopping. · il faut que + faire, subjunctive, on

Practice: subjunctive from the ils form
  1. ils {prenne*|prennent} → il faut qu'on ___ qu'on prenne
  2. ils {vienne*|viennent} → j'aimerais que tu ___ que tu viennes
  3. ils‿{attende*|attendent} → il faut que j'___ que j'attende
  4. ils‿{écrive*|écrivent} → pour qu'il ___ qu'il écrive
  5. faire → il faut qu'on ___ qu'on fasse (one of the six)
